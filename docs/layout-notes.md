# Bay station PCB -- placement notes (placement only, nothing routed)

Status 2026-11-18 (second pass: placement refinement). `bay-station/bay-station.kicad_pcb` carries the
board outline, 4 M3 holes, 3 fiducials and a position for all 428 footprints. **There are no tracks, no vias
and no filled zones.** Everything is generated by one idempotent script (two consecutive runs give a
byte-identical board, md5 checked; `.kicad_pro` is restored byte for byte):

```
/Applications/KiCad/KiCad.app/Contents/Frameworks/Python.framework/Versions/Current/bin/python3 \
    scripts/layout/place_bay_station.py [--dump layout.json] [--out other.kicad_pcb] [--no-save]   # ~90 s
python3 scripts/layout/layout_metrics.py layout.json [--worst 30]      # HPWL/MST/crossings, clock + RF paths, worst parts
python3 scripts/layout/render_layout.py layout.json out.png [--crop x0 y0 x1 y1] [--nets uwb] [--labels]
```

The script resets nothing it does not own: it keeps the footprints, nets, 4-layer stackup and the
`.kicad_pro` (restored after `BOARD.Save()`, which otherwise wipes the netclasses; memory note
*kicad-pcbnew-headless-gotchas*) and seeds the KIIDs. Hand-placed parts (ICs, connectors, SMA, RF chains,
mechanical) are the `FIXED` table plus `chain_positions()`. Everything else is placed in two stages:

1. **Constructive pass** (`place_all`): a deterministic greedy search that pulls each part toward the pin it
   serves, inside a per-block region, on a 0.25 mm grid (test pads 1.25 mm), courtyards inflated by 0.2 mm so
   neighbouring courtyards are >= 0.4 mm apart (was 0.6 mm in the first pass; the 0.1 mm per part bought about
   10 % of total wirelength, because the pin neighbourhoods were the bottleneck). Order of priority: clock
   R/C pairs (22R at the buffer pin, 2200 pF at the XTI pin) -> DW3210 supply pins -> sensors -> nRF9151
   supply, GNSS/LTE front end, SIM, +VBAT_LTE hot loop, 3V3 buck, charger -> **all test pads (127 in this run; 62 since 2026-11-18)** (so they
   claim a slot beside their pin before the low-priority series links, pull-ups, DNP options and UI parts do)
   -> the rest.
2. **Polish** (`polish`, 6 passes): every greedily placed part except the clock tree is lifted out and re-seated,
   in all four rotations, at the legal pose that minimises the MST length of the nets it sits on (exact for
   nets of <= 9 pads, nearest-pad distance for the large rails), plus the original pin-hint pull; RF nets
   (LTE/GNSS feed, LNA) count 4x. A move is taken only if it shortens that cost, so the result is
   deterministic. It moved 170/69/29/17/8/3 parts in the six passes.

Coordinates below are board-local mm, origin at the top-left corner, x east, y south. The board
origin sits at (60, 50) on the KiCad sheet.

## Block rationale

### UWB array (`uwb_array_notes.md`, `positioning.md`)

* **Why 2 x 2 with SMA on two edges, not a row of four.** Clock-path equality was the deciding
  metric: 1 cm of differential path is ~10 cm of position error at 10 m (positioning.md; CLAUDE.md).
  A row of four on one edge forces buffer-to-XTI paths that differ by one chip pitch (>= 22 mm with
  the cluster width the decoupling needs). A brute-force search over the 24 anchor-to-slot
  assignments x 4 buffer rotations (done offline, result frozen in `SLOT`) gave the 2 x 2 layout the
  shortest, non-crossing tree with a path spread of ~10 mm instead of ~22. The price is two coax
  exits (north and south) and a block that splits the board into west (UWB) and east (host/power);
  west-east nets cross through the centre band.
* **Shared SPI/RST/WAKEUP/SYNC bus (the heaviest connection on the board).** The four chips sit at the corners of
  a 22 x 40 mm rectangle, so a 4-drop net cannot be shorter than its Steiner tree of ~80-100 mm whatever
  the host does; the shared nets measure 70-102 mm MST each (was 94-113). Two things were checked and
  neither helps enough: (a) **U20 position** -- a sweep of all four rotations over x 10-80, y 16-54 on
  the 14 bus/IRQ/CS nets shows the cost is flat in y and rises ~13 mm of ratsnest per mm of x east of the
  array, and that rotation 0 (UWB pins facing west, as now) is the best of the four at the host's x; going
  west would pay (x = 54 instead of 66: -180 mm) but the host cluster does not fit: a trial that pulled U20,
  the BT840 and the host regions 4-6 mm west/north raised total MST from 3.76 k to 4.4-4.6 k mm because
  the SIM/regulator/test-pad parts then spilled across the array edge (tried before the polish stage and the
  0.2 mm courtyard gap existed; **not retried since**, worth one more attempt with a larger board). (b)
  **Pin facing**: the DW3210 bus pins (31-37) are on the side opposite the RF pins, i.e. they face the
  centre band for all four chips, and the per-anchor IRQ (pin 1) and SYNC (pin 9) are on the west side of
  the rot 0 chips, east side of the rot 180 chips; the order along the pin row is mirrored between north and
  south chips (rot 180), which is why the ratsnest of the 6 shared nets forms two overlapping X-shaped
  bundles instead of six lanes. A mirrored footprint would fix that but means B.Cu parts, which the brief rules
  out. The per-anchor CS/IRQ lines are 22-44 mm (A1/A2 the longest: they are the west column).
  Net effect of the second pass on the bus: 6 shared nets 648 -> 589 mm MST, 28 -> 25 crossings; all 33 bus nets
  1098 -> 1001 mm, crossings 144 -> 144.
* **RF path (identical on all four).** Straight line chip RF1 pin 18 -> C47 (2.0 pF) -> R220 (0R)
  -> J2x pad 1, with the DNP pi shunts C200/C201 beside the line, all 0402 per
  `uwb_array_notes.md` "RF front end". Estimated pad-centre chain length is **9.02 mm for A0, A1, A2
  and A3** (spread 0.00 mm); the north chains are the 180-degree image of the south chains. RF2 pin
  13 gets its 49R9 (R33-R36) directly below/above the pin. Nothing but the 0402s sits in the
  1.5 mm channel between chip and SMA, so a 50 ohm line of the stackup calculator's width fits.
  The chain stays 4.25 mm from the chip centre outward so no part touches the chip courtyard.
* **Clock tree (`clock_dist_notes.md` "Layout").** R231-R234 (22R) sit at the buffer output pins and C24-C27
  (2200 pF) at each XTI pin, placed *first* (before any other decoupling) so they get the free copper at the
  pins. U31 was moved from (26, 37) to (24.5, 35) (rot 90 as before) by a pose search: the straight-line
  distance from each buffer output pin to its XTI pin is then 23.0-23.8 mm instead of 20.6-27.9 mm (the
  netlist fixes which output feeds which anchor, so only the pose of the buffer can be chosen). Straight
  buffer-pin -> R -> C -> XTI estimates (pad centres), A0..A3: **28.1 / 26.9 / 28.8 / 24.0 mm**, was
  30.4 / 28.8 / 34.4 / 24.7, so every path is shorter and the spread fell from 9.7 to 4.8 mm. **The remaining
  spread still has to be closed with a meander on A3 (~4.7 mm) and A1 (~1.9 mm) at routing**; a fixed
  mismatch calibrates out, a long unequal run that drifts with temperature does not. Ratsnest crossings in the
  tree: 0; tree MST 141 -> 128 mm. TCXO output pin to U31 CLKIN is 6.6 mm. (A pose with spread 3.2 mm was
  found, but it left A3 at 25.3 mm, 0.6 mm longer than the first pass, so it was rejected.)
* **Decoupling (Fig 32 via the `Placement` fields in `uwb_array.kicad_sch`).** Per anchor the greedy
  pass pulls C45 to VTX_D pin 27, C41 to VDD2a pin 28 (FB after it), C40 to VDD1 pin 29, C42/C43/C44
  to VDD3 pin 26 (smallest first), C46 to VIO_D pin 38, C28 to XTO pin 22 and C24 to XTI pin 21,
  all inside a 25 x 20 mm box around the chip. Pad-to-pin distance (pad centres): 2.1-6.1 mm, mean 3.75
  mm (first pass: up to 6.8 mm, mean 4.1); the VTX_D, VIO_D, XTO and XTI caps are 2.1-3.5 mm, the 4.7 uF/10 uF
  VDD3 parts are the furthest (4.0-6.1 mm; the 0603 courtyard is 3 x 1.5 mm and the pin's neighbours are
  occupied). Verify against Fig 32 once routed.
* **No copper under the chip.** Rule areas `KO_DW3210_An_body` forbid tracks, vias and pours
  under each body on F.Cu and In1.Cu (decisions.md says the Fig 35 photo shows In1 cleared only
  under the RF half; the brief asked for both layers, so both are blocked -- relax In1 if routing
  needs it).
* **A0 fallback crystal Y1 (DNP)** and C208/C209 are at U4's XTI/XTO pins.

### Clock sources

TCXO U30 is the 15.6 x 10.6 mm PLACEHOLDER footprint (Rakon RPT1490LN, quote-only part). Pin 4
(output) faces the buffer; the pad geometry is unverified, so re-check the 6.6 mm output run when the

### Magnetometer U10 / accelerometer U11 (decisions.md "Environment and georeferencing")

U11 is 5.5 mm from U10. Measured from the placed board (pad to pad; guideline >= 25 mm from the buck inductor
and its switching loop, >= 20 mm from the LTE feed / +VBAT_LTE and bulk caps): U10 is **66.5 mm from L1**
(65.2 for U11), **53.6 mm from the whole L1 switching loop** (U8, C21, C22, R8, R300, R301, J30, TP400/402),
**50.2 mm from L300** and 44.8 mm from the U40 + L300 loop, **24.0 mm from the nearest +VBAT_LTE part**
(C100/C101; the larger bulk caps C300-C302 are 43-62 mm away), 39.8 mm from the LTE u.FL J10 (which now sits
beside the nRF) and 22.1 mm from the nRF9151 package edge. 20.3 mm from the TCXO can, >= 31 mm from every M3 hole,
>= 36 mm from the SIM shell, 80+ mm from the jacks and battery. **The +VBAT_LTE and +VSYS routes must be kept >= 20 mm
from U10 at routing** (U40 is 46.5 mm from U10; keep the U40 -> nRF9151 feed east of x = 60). The 25/20 mm figures are
engineering guidance, not ST numbers. Moving U10/U11 east to shorten the 80-97 mm I2C nets is possible only by
~6 mm before the 20 mm +VBAT_LTE guideline binds, so it was not done.

### Host: nRF9151 U20, BT840 U3, SIM, RF (`connectivity_notes.md`)

* U20 is rotated 0: its UWB pins (67-80) face west toward the array, the BT840 UART pins face north
  toward U3, SIM/SWD/reset pins face south, LTE (pin 35) faces east. U3 is rotated 0 with its
  antenna end flush with the north edge (antenna assumed at the module's -Y end, from the pad field
  offset in `Fanstel_BT840_Module_14x16mm_16Cast_49LGA`); no other part is within 10 mm of the
  antenna end in x or in the antenna rectangle.
* **J10 (LTE) and J11 (GNSS) u.FL are interior parts now, beside the nRF9151 RF pins** (J10 (79.5, 25.5), 8.4 mm
  from pin 35; J11 (82.5, 14), 14.8 mm from pin 42), instead of on the north edge 22-29 mm away. The footprint is
  `U.FL_Hirose_U.FL-R-SMT-1_Vertical`: a vertical-mating (from above) receptacle, so the pigtail leaves
  upward/sideways over the board and no edge is needed (not checked against the Hirose drawing, only the
  footprint name and its symmetric courtyard; verify). The pi at J10 (R130/C130/C131/D101) is at the connector as
  `connectivity_notes.md` asks. The GNSS chain is U24 (LNA, (78, 17)) and U25 (SAW, (73, 16)): ratsnest pin 42 -> U25 4.1 mm, U25 -> U24 10.5 mm,
  U24 -> J11 9.6 mm; the LTE pin -> pi -> J10 is 5.5 + 5.3 mm (first pass: 12 + 10 mm of nets, 22 mm straight). The
  RF lines still need a 50 ohm line and ground stitching. SW11 (BT840 reset) moved to (92, 17) to make room.
* U40 (+VBAT_LTE) is east of the nRF9151: U40 VOUT -> R302 -> FB10 -> VDD is 22 mm (pad centres); its hot loop
  U40-L300 is 3.8 mm pad to pad on both switch pins.
* SIM J12 (80, 48) is 13 mm from the nRF9151 SIM pins, contact row toward the modem (rot 0: the six contact
  pads are along the -Y end). **J12 is a hinged nano-SIM socket** (GCT SIM8060-6-0-14-00, datasheet
  `gct.co/files/drawings/sim8060.pdf`, "Hinged Type, SMT, 6 Pin, 1.43 mm profile"; instructions: slide the
  metal lid from LOCK to OPEN, open the lid, place the card chip-face down, close and slide to LOCK), so
  the card is loaded from above, not slid in from an edge; the first pass's "slot direction" worry does not
  apply. What it needs instead is ~14 x 12.4 mm of free height above the part for the lid swing and slide, which
  is an enclosure decision. U22 (SIM ESD) is between J12 and the nRF. The DNP eSIM U23 is to its west.
* Programming: J3 (SWD) is 6.4 mm from nRF9151 SWDIO; J34, J33 beside it. BT840's J32/J35 are east
  of the module next to its SWD pins. SW11 (RESET_BLE) now sits at (92, 17), no longer beside them (it makes
  room for the u.FL connectors; its net is one wire to the BT840 reset); SW2 (RESET_MCU) beside J3.

### Power (`power_bms_notes.md`, `regulation_notes.md`)

Flow runs east edge -> west: J1/J2 (east edge) -> D1/D2 -> U1 charger (112, 34) -> +VSYS -> U8
(104, 48) and U40 (84.5, 33) -> +3V3 west to the array. BT1 JST is on the east edge (battery cable
leaves the board), Q1/D3/U2 next to it. U8: SW pin -> L1 is 3.6 mm and L1 -> C21 2.4 mm (pad centres; 8.8 mm total in the first pass), input cap C20 2.4 mm from
VIN; the feedback divider R8 is by the output, not adjacent to the SW pin (its FB distance was not re-measured). Charger LEDs D4-D6 are on
the south edge with their resistors. LEDs D7/D400/D401 and SW12 are on the north edge east of
the GNSS parts where a lid can show them (**enclosure not designed: all UI positions are
provisional**).

### Test pads (`verification-plan.md` test-point index)

**Superseded 2026-11-18: the pads were cut from 127 to 62 and move to the bottom side in function rows (`scripts/layout/testpoint_labels.json`); the board grows to 150 x 80 mm. The numbers below describe the earlier 127-pad, top-side placement and are kept as a record, not as current.**

127 pads, D1.0 (107) and D1.5 (20), all on F.Cu, on a 1.25 mm grid; minimum centre distance 3.75 mm
(>= 2.5 mm probe spacing). All pads are placed right after the pin-critical parts (see the stage list), each
pulled to the pin or net it measures (explicit targets for the rail and ground-spring pads: TP3/TP110 at the
clock, TP111/TP112 beside TP10/TP12, TP101 at the far end of the array, TP102 at the nRF9151/BT840 end), then the
swap pass and the polish pass (all pads re-seated within 10 mm, cost = MST increase of their net, so a pad on the
natural route of a net costs nothing). TP402/TP411/TP412 are switch-node pads: **routing must put them on the
switch copper with no stub or delete them** (`regulation_notes.md` section 7). Result: the 127 pads add **548 mm** of
ratsnest (first pass 737 mm); mean distance from a pad to the nearest other pin of its net 6.7 mm (7.6), 59
pads further than 5 mm (77), worst 23.5 mm. The far ones are on nets whose pins sit in packed
neighbourhoods: COEX2/DEC0/GNSS_LNA_EN (nRF pins on the crowded east side), SIM_IO/CLK/RST/1V8, UWB_CS1/IRQ1/IRQ2, ACC_INT2, BRIDGE_IRQ, SWDCLK_BLE (12-23 mm). Because they are
free-standing 2.1 mm courtyards, the supply of free slots beside a pin, not the search, is the limit; they are the
first thing to trim if the board needs room. The 28 anchor GPIO/EXTON pads (TP501..TP527) are 2-pin nets
right at the chip pins.

## Rotations and conventions

ICs and connectors use 0/90/180/270 (connectors turn their mating face toward the edge, see "Connectors"). The two-pad
passives are free to take all four rotations in the polish pass and choose the one that puts pad 1 (the signal/pin
side of the decoupling and series parts) at the pin and pad 2 at the return/rail; that gave 81/67/44/43 parts at
0/90/270/180 (235 parts), every position on the 0.25 mm grid (0 off-grid), test pads on the 1.25 mm grid. The vertical RF
series parts (C47, R220) and RF2 terminations (R33-R36) are at 270 (south chips) / 90 (north chips) so pad 1 faces the chip,
and the north pi shunts (C202/C203/C206/C207) are at 180 as the mirror of the south ones. **Not normalised:** the
decoupling caps around one DW3210 are not all one orientation (U4: 6 at 0, 3 at 90, 1 at 270; U5/U7 4 at 90, 3 at 0) because each
follows the side of the chip its pin is on; pad 1 is toward the pin in every case. A silk pass that wants a uniform
polarity convention will have to flip pad 2/pad 1 explicitly, which the placement does not do.

## Not verified / assumptions to check (flag, not invention)

1. **BT1 (JST PH side entry) mating direction** is from the footprint geometry only (JST's PDF was not
   reachable). J1 and J2 are confirmed from the vendor drawings (see "Connectors"); J12 is hinged, so there is no
   edge to point at, but its lid hinge side and the clearance above it are unverified.
2. **SMA footprint is a PLACEHOLDER** (`SMA_Linx_CONSMA020.042-G_EdgeMount_PLACEHOLDER`): the
   1.52 mm centre pad is far wider than a 50 ohm line and the connector is rated for 1.07 mm
   board vs this 1.048 mm stackup. The launch and ground cut-back need EM simulation at 6.5 GHz.
   The chain lengths above are pad-centre estimates, not electrical lengths.
3. **BT840 antenna side and keep-out size** are inferred from the pad field and a conservative
   10 mm side clearance; Fanstel's drawing was not re-read here.
4. **DW3210 land pattern and the Fig 35 keep-out** are still the unresolved items in decisions.md;
   the 4 x 4 mm rule area is a placement-stage guard, not the verified copper rule.
5. **TCXO, MAX17048 and eSIM footprints are placeholders**, so their courtyards (and U30's ~7 mm
   output run) may change.
6. **Clock-path spread of 4.8 mm** (28.1 / 26.9 / 28.8 / 24.0) still needs a meander on A3 and A1 at routing; nothing
   was routed to prove the equal-length goal, and the numbers are straight pad-centre estimates.
7. **The u.FL connectors are assumed vertical-mating** (footprint name), so that they can sit beside the nRF9151 instead
   of on an edge; if the lid/enclosure needs them at an edge the 22 mm runs come back. The GNSS LNA run is 10.5 mm.
8. **No 3D clearance check**: TCXO height, jack, SIM lid and u.FL against an enclosure lid are open
   (the enclosure is not designed). The LEDs, buttons and their distance to the nRF9151 are provisional for the same reason.
9. **Hole grounding, LED/button locations, header orientations** follow the open decisions and were
   placed for access, not against an enclosure drawing.
10. Test pads were placed for proximity, not yet reviewed against how a spring probe approaches
    each cluster; 59 of 127 are > 5 mm from the nearest pin of their net because the neighbourhoods are full.
11. **Host cluster west shift** (U20 at x = 54-60 would cut ~13 mm of ratsnest per mm) failed at the 0.3 mm courtyard
    gap in the first pass of this refinement; it was not retried with the polish stage and a larger board.
12. **Courtyard gap is 0.4 mm** (0.2 mm per part); the silk pass and via stitching were sized for 0.6 mm, so check the
    silk and stitching-via clearances again.
