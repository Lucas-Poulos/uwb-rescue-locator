# Bay-station silkscreen: what the script puts on the board, and why

Generated by `scripts/layout/silkscreen.py` (run it on the PLACED board; it is
generic and idempotent), checked by its built-in checker and by
`scripts/layout/fab_checks.py`. `bay-station.kicad_pcb` itself is not edited by
the tooling commit; run the script in place after placement is final:

```
PY=/Applications/KiCad/KiCad.app/Contents/Frameworks/Python.framework/Versions/Current/bin/python3
$PY scripts/layout/silkscreen.py bay-station/bay-station.kicad_pcb --log /tmp/silk.log
$PY scripts/layout/fab_checks.py bay-station/bay-station.kicad_pcb
```

It saves the board with `BOARD.Save()` and restores the `.kicad_pro` bytes
afterwards (Save() otherwise wipes the netclasses). Everything it creates sits in
the board group `SILK_AUTO` or is an `OrientationMark_*` footprint and is
rebuilt on every run (two consecutive runs give a byte-identical file; KIIDs are
seeded). Close the KiCad GUI first. Re-run after any placement change.

## Fab rules used

JLCPCB PCB capabilities page (jlcpcb.com/capabilities/pcb-capabilities, read
2026-11-18): silkscreen line width **>= 0.15 mm**, text height **>= 1.0 mm**
(40 mil), width:height about 1:6, silkscreen-to-pad clearance **>= 0.15 mm**.
The page states **no** silkscreen-to-board-edge or -to-hole clearance; the
0.5 mm planning / 0.3 mm check edge margin in the script is this project's own
choice. KiCad also clips silk at mask openings when plotting, so native footprint
outlines touching a pad do not reach the Gerber.

## Rules

| Item | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Reference designators | F.SilkS, centred, 1.0 mm / 0.15 stroke, angle 0 or 90 only (reads from the bottom or the right). Obstacles considered by the placer: every pad (with mask expansion), vias,
all native footprint silk (graphics and text), neighbours' courtyards, previously
placed generated items, and the board edge.

## Orientation glyph footprints

New pinless, courtyard-less, BOM/pos-excluded footprints in
`bay-station/libs/bay_station.pretty` (built by
`scripts/layout/build_orientation_marks.py`; already inside the registered
`bay_station` library, so no lib-table change):

* `OrientationMark_Axes_IIS2DLPC` (U11): X down along the pin 1-4 edge, Y right
  along the pin 1-12-11-10 edge, Z out of the board (dot in circle), with the
  U11 label. **Source:** ST DocID031788 Rev 4, Figure 2 (p.9), "TOP VIEW,
  direction of the detectable accelerations", read from the rendered figure:
  pin-1 dot on the back corner, X toward the lower left, Y toward the lower
  right, Z up, a right-handed set. KiCad's `LGA-12_2x2mm_P0.5mm` has pad 1 at
  top-left with pads 1-4 down the left edge (checked from the pad coordinates).
* `OrientationMark_Axes_IIS2MDC` (U10): **UNVERIFIED.** DocID030986 Rev 1,
  Figure 2 (p.7) shows a pin-1 dot with Y toward the lower left and X toward
  the lower right, i.e. Y along the pin 1-4 edge and X along the pin
  1-12-11-10 edge. With Z drawn up that triad is **left-handed**, so Z is
  deliberately not drawn and the axes should be confirmed (e.g. rotate a
  magnet at bring-up) before firmware relies on them.
* `OrientationMark_StationFrame`: FWD arrow. `docs/positioning.md` ("Mechanical")
  says only that A0 is offset forward out of the A1-A3 plane; it defines **no**
  axes, heading zero, or which board edge faces forward. The arrow points toward
  the top of the board by default (`--fwd <deg CCW>` to change): **placeholder**
  until that mechanical decision is made. Heading zero of the magnetometer
  relative to this arrow is likewise undefined.

The script places each sensor glyph with the sensor's rotation so the arrows stay
parallel to the real axes, nearest free spot (up to 14 mm). If a glyph cannot be
placed it is dropped with a log line; the footprint can also be placed by hand.
Because the glyphs have no schematic symbol, KiCad's "Update PCB from schematic"
will offer to delete them unless "Delete footprints with no symbols" is
unticked; the repo's `ORIENTATION_MARK` pinless symbol could carry them if they
should be schematic-owned (not done here).

## Checker

`silkscreen.py` ends with a checker (`--check-only` to run it alone) using
pcbnew shapes: silk text/graphics vs pad shapes (0.15 mm), vs other silk, vs
vias and vs Edge.Cuts (0.3 mm). Generated violations are removed
(references go to F.Fab) and logged until the checker is clean; library-native
silk touching pads is counted separately (`native_silk_pad`) because the script
does not edit library geometry.
`fab_checks.py` works on a scratch copy with the three silk DRC rules forced to
warning, reports `silk_over_copper` / `silk_overlap` / `silk_edge_clearance`,
lists references without readable silk, and dry-runs Gerber + drill + position
export (nothing written in the repo).

All 22 `silk_over_copper` are **library-native** silk of the three PLACEHOLDER
footprints (U30 Rakon, U2 MAX17048, U23 eSIM) touching their own pads; none come
from generated items. They go away when those footprints are replaced.
B is a deliberately cramped stress case: most refs cannot fit at 1.0 mm in the
gaps and go to F.Fab, as the rule says.

## Not verified / open

* IIS2MDC axes and Z direction (above). IIS2DLPC axes are read from the figure,
  not from a mechanical drawing of the die.
* Station FWD direction and frame axes (positioning.md defines none).
* J1 `CENTRE +`: taken from the schematic (pad 1 = tip = `BARREL_VIN`, `power_bms_notes.md` "Pin 1 = tip/+"); the CUI PJ-063AH drawing was not checked for which pad is the centre pin. `5V ONLY` follows the MCP73871 VIN limit note (`power_bms_notes.md`), not a measured spec.
* LED cathode orientation vs the real Kingbright parts (open note in `indicators.kicad_sch`).
* D4 `CHG` / D5 `FULL` / D6 `PG` wording comes from the sheet's truth table; D4 also means low-battery on some MCP73871 variants.
* BT840 antenna region is taken from the footprint description; keep clear of copper below it per Fanstel datasheet p.16 (not re-read here).
* JLCPCB silk-to-edge and silk-to-hole minima are not published on the capabilities page; 0.5 mm edge margin is this project's choice.
* Text at 0.8 mm is below JLC's 1.0 mm height minimum; `fab_checks.py` lists every such reference.
* U23 (eSIM placeholder) has no pad named 1, so it gets no pin-1 dot.
